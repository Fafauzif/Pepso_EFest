# What would change our mind?

This is a prospective decision aid, not a summary of primary results. H1–H11 follow the closure plan's initial explanations. Adapt identifiers if the final synthesis matrix uses a different scheme; the mechanism names are the stable meanings.

The aim is to distinguish plausible explanations in a purchase sequence. A participant's account can support several mechanisms at once. Do not force every case to have one root cause. A disconfirming case can narrow the conditions under which a mechanism applies without proving it never occurs.

## Evidence categories for each hypothesis update

- **Supports mechanism in this event:** the sequence and interpretation fit, with source and uncertainty stated.
- **Counterexample:** a condition expected to produce the behaviour was present but the behaviour differed, or a predicted step was absent.
- **Rival explanation:** another mechanism fits the event at least as well.
- **Insufficient:** key variant, chronology, role, price, alternative, or outcome is unknown.
- **Outside scope:** useful context but not target-age, country, case-period, tier, or first-person evidence for the specific claim.

Keep confidence in **mechanism plausibility** separate from confidence in **application to the target group**. Use explanatory labels and reasons, not a numerical probability manufactured from interview counts.

## When a conditional strategy direction becomes reviewable

Before turning a direction into a proposed pilot, specify:

1. The person and purchase situation it is intended to change.
2. The mechanism that must be true and the evidence/alternative explanations still unresolved.
3. The behavioural outcome: e.g., completed next replenishment of the intended SKU at a documented paid price; distinguish intention, trial, repeat, volume, and share of mix.
4. The relevant comparison and follow-up horizon, plus availability and payer conditions.
5. Economic conditions: incremental contribution, cannibalization, channel/service costs, and who benefits or pays.
6. A stopping or revision rule if the mechanism does not appear, uptake does not become repeat, or the intervention merely shifts existing Premium buyers between channels/discounts.

No primary sample in this kit can by itself estimate causal sales uplift, willingness-to-pay distributions, or population retention. Qualitative results should sharpen the next test and identify where an appealing idea would fail.
