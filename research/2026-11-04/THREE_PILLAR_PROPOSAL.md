# Proposed three-pillar architecture — 4 November 2026

Status: analyst proposal requested by the user, not user-accepted strategy or validated intervention. No new external facts, primary data, age-prevalence estimates, partnerships, budgets or uplift estimates.

## Segmentation

Use purchase control, experienced value and actual replacement friction as the primary routing variables. Treat ages 18–24 and 25–30 as recruitment/reporting cuts only; do not assert an age threshold or make older adults ineligible for household routes. Household-supplied use, shared decisions and independent purchases can occur across the case age range. Public spouse-choice and family-benefit accounts provide counterexamples to equating household influence with students.

## Umbrella objective

Increase appropriate paid Premium replacement among eligible 18–30 former Premium users, with incremental contribution relative to usual practice. Distinguish Pepsodent and total-Unilever incrementality. Accept satisfied Basic users without a valued specialist need as outside the immediate conversion target.

## Pillar 1: Carry the user's choice into the household purchase

Audience: user values a suitable Premium variant but another person supplies the next tube. Proposed mechanism: user creates a specific, shareable shopping item with exact variant/pack, supported benefit, current available price and preferred outlet. Household buyer can incorporate it into the normal shopping trip, optionally as a personal tube rather than an expensive household-wide replacement. No mandatory bundle or assumption of savings. Build through existing brand/retail touchpoints, conditional on integration access. Test whether choice actually reaches the chooser/payer and results in sustained funded replacement; stop if household roles do not constrain choice or extra basket cost dominates.

## Pillar 2: Establish a personally valued reason to repeat

Audience: former users with a relevant need but uncertain variant match, disappointing expectations or unsuitable sensory experience. Proposed mechanism: use existing specialist content/tools or trained assistance to match need, explain supported benefits and realistic evaluation conditions, then check whether a useful experience actually occurs. No universal time-to-result, new scanner, clinical diagnosis from a quiz, or reward for positive reviews. If unsuitable or symptoms need care, allow exit or professional referral. Test appropriate selection plus subsequent paid choice; stop if comprehension improves but no valued experience or purchase change follows.

## Pillar 3: Replace before the household fallback takes over

Audience: users who experienced a valued benefit but lapse because of forgetting, exact-SKU friction or a temporary re-entry barrier. Proposed mechanism: optional depletion-timed reminder, exact-variant purchase route and transparent existing pack economics. Test a limited return offer only for suitable lapsed users; compare usual practice, reminder/access support, and reminder/access plus incentive. Check the following replacement without an additional program subsidy. Do not infer regular-price willingness from redemption or prohibit ordinary retailer deals. Sustained affordability failure can disqualify a route from scaling. Do not promise real-time stock without retailer integration.

## Red-team changes incorporated

- Replaced age-exclusive pillars with behavioural routing.
- Replaced a household-wide Premium bundle with optional beneficiary-specific purchase coordination.
- Replaced generic education/new AI scanner with demonstrated selection/expectation/experience repair using existing capabilities.
- Replaced blanket win-back coupons with a limited, incrementality-tested incentive arm and subsequent replacement check.
- Avoid mandatory subscriptions, fixed depletion schedules, invented demand, clinical benefit claims or guaranteed stock.
- Count unique users and purchases once across pillars; intermediate indicators are not incremental revenue.
